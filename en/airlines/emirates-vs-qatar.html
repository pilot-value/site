CAREER GUIDE

Which Should a Japanese Pilot Choose? Three Profiles

Emirates pays somewhat more — a gap of roughly ¥800万 — but both are tax-free and both sit far above Japanese pay. Which is exactly why the airline you pick comes down to what you put first.

1
The family is coming, and their education comes first
If you want Japanese schooling, a Japanese community and Japanese-speaking healthcare in one place
Emirates (Dubai) has the edge

Dubai has built a community of more than 20,000 Japanese residents, with Japanese-language education, Japanese food and Japanese-speaking clinics all in place. For moving with children, the peace of mind is greater than in Qatar.

2
You want in early and want to build the career fast
Your hours are still light, or you want to make your Gulf debut sooner rather than later
Start with Qatar Airways

The hiring bar sits lower than Emirates and you can apply from 3,000 hours. There really are Japanese pilots who build wide-body time at Qatar first, then move across to Emirates.

3
You want retirement assets to hold up after you stop flying
You are trying to fit a Gulf career into a long-term plan
Both carry a trap worth watching

The gratuity is thinner than a Japanese retirement lump sum. While you are working in the Gulf you sit outside Japan's employees' pension scheme, so retirement assets hollow out easily. Making use of iDeCo and voluntary-contribution schemes is essential.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Which pays more, an Emirates or a Qatar Airways pilot?+
An Emirates captain earns roughly 3,700万円 and a Qatar captain roughly 2,900万円, so Emirates is somewhat ahead — a gap of about 800万円. But because both are tax-free, converting to Japanese taxed terms puts Emirates at the equivalent of 6,400万円, far above anything available in Japan. Emirates also tends to lead on base pay and on total compensation once housing and education allowances are counted.
Is Gulf pilot pay really tax-free?+
The UAE and Qatar levy no personal income tax. Earn 3,700万円 in Japan and income tax plus residence tax leave you with about 2,200万円; in the Gulf the full 3,700万円 stays in your pocket. If you fly out while keeping a registered address in Japan you can still fall within Japanese taxation, so completing the transfer of your resident registration matters.
Which is easier for a Japanese pilot to be hired by, Emirates or Qatar?+
Both screen on English (ICAO Level 4 or above) and on total flight time. Emirates looks for 4,000+ hours, Qatar for 3,000+. Judged on the number of places and the competition ratio, Qatar hires more and the door for entry is wider. Plenty of Japanese pilots take the route of proving themselves at Qatar first and moving to Emirates afterwards.
Dubai or Doha — which is the better place to live?+
Dubai is a global city with a large Japanese community and strong Japanese food and Japanese-language education. Entertainment and dining are abundant, which makes it ideal for anyone who wants living abroad to feel less like living abroad. Doha is smaller in scale but safe, with a slightly lower cost of living. It is culturally conservative, so restrictions on alcohol are worth noting. The pattern is Dubai if the family is coming, Doha if you prefer a simpler life.
What happens to a Gulf pilot's retirement?+
Severance is thinner than in Japan (a gratuity scheme: years of service × monthly salary). Most pilots go home when the contract expires, and unless they keep contributing to a Japanese pension, retirement funding can become a worry. Voluntary enrollment in the National Pension is possible even while living abroad, so keeping up the monthly contributions matters. Design an investment plan around that high income — iDeCo, NISA, offshore accounts — as early as you can.
