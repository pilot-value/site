The amounts above are different kinds of figure. Emirates is the recruitment example the airline publishes itself (take home cash, and a pay-and-benefits total that includes housing, schooling and other items). Etihad Airways is the ceiling stated in its job posting (based on 75 flying hours a month; a maximum, not an average). Qatar Airways and Gulf Air have no official figure, so those cells carry our own estimate, worked out from public information and not a figure the airline publishes. The Riyadh Air figure is calculated from pay data submitted anonymously to PILOT VALUE together with public material, and it is not a figure released by the airline. Because they are different kinds of figure, they cannot be added, subtracted or ranked against one another. Flight hours, English levels, housing, annual leave and vacancies are as stated on each airline’s own pages and job postings. "None" means the airline publishes nothing for that item. Amounts are treated as having no income tax deducted.

READING THE OFFICIAL NUMBERS

Emirates publishes three figures, and which one you call "salary" changes it by more than 2×

Emirates publishes more detail than any other Gulf carrier. But the three figures it lists do not add up to one another. An article that gives a single "Emirates captains earn X" does not say which of the three it means.

What each of the three figures covers, per the conditions on the Emirates page
  • Take home cash — basic salary and flying pay, annualised at 85 flying hours a month.
  • Pay & benefits — the cash above plus accommodation, education, medical, travel and provident fund, and it assumes "employee, spouse and 2 eligible children". Someone without children does not receive the same amount.
  • Accommodation value — the value of the housing provided in kind. It does not arrive as cash.

The Emirates amounts in the table above are two of these three — take home cash, and pay and benefits — converted into yen. What this site collects from pilots is gross pay as printed on a payslip, which does not include company housing or school fees. Because the two count different things, the airline's published figures and pilots' own payslip figures are set side by side with the kind of figure named, rather than one replacing the other.

ETIHAD

Etihad states an "up to" figure, with its conditions, in the job posting

First Officer

“with the potential to earn up to AED 513,732 (USD 138,318) per year based on 75 flying hours per month”
Source: Etihad Airways job posting (first officer)
  • What it contains: basic salary, flying pay, housing allowance, and layover and meal allowances.
  • What it excludes: education allowance (AED 40,000 primary, AED 55,000 secondary, up to three children), income protection, insurance, staff travel, end-of-service benefit and relocation support — all listed separately.
  • Requirements: 2,000 hours total with 1,500 hours multi-crew glass cockpit and 500 hours on type (criteria A), or 1,500 hours total with 1,000 hours on A320 (criteria B). A320 flying within the last 12 months, ICAO ATPL (frozen accepted), class one medical, under 50 on joining, ICAO English Level 4+. 42 days annual leave.

Captain

“up to AED 674,029 (USD 181,589) per year based on 75 flying hours per month”
Source: Etihad Airways job posting (captain, ref 80371)
  • Requirements: 5,500 hours total, 2,500 hours PIC multi-crew glass cockpit, 1,500 hours PIC on A320. A320 flying within the last 12 months, ATPL, class one medical, under 59 on joining, ICAO English Level 4+. Augmented flights are counted at 75%, stated explicitly. 42 days annual leave.
  • This "up to" figure is a ceiling, based on 75 flying hours a month. It is not an average and not a guaranteed minimum. 75 hours a month is close to the top of what is actually flown, so an ordinary month lands below it. Separately listed items such as education support and the end-of-service benefit are not inside this figure either.
Housing in kind and a cash housing allowance are not the same item

Emirates provides accommodation in kind and states its value apart from pay. Etihad pays a cash housing allowance, which sits inside the "up to AED 513,732". So although both are "housing included", the Emirates take-home-cash figure excludes housing while the Etihad figure includes it. Add or subtract across the two and one of them always comes out wrong. This is the main reason aggregator comparison tables of the Gulf disagree with each other.

Etihad also publishes cadet pay as monthly amounts — starting at AED 5,000, then 16,667, then 24,000, capped at 34,000, and AED 39,853 on appointment as senior first officer. These are monthly, not annual, so they cannot be lined up directly against other carriers’ annual figures.

WHERE NOTHING OFFICIAL EXISTS

Qatar Airways, Riyadh Air and Gulf Air — neither pay nor entry requirements published

These three put out neither a pay figure nor their entry requirements, so the pay cells above carry our own estimate. Some other sites do quote figures, but none of them says where the figure came from, so they are not used here.

QR

Qatar Airways — pay and entry requirements both unpublished

Qatar Airways publishes neither pay nor entry requirements. Flight hours, English level, age limits and housing are all absent from its official pages, which is why those cells are empty in the table above. The figures of $182K for a captain and $129K for a first officer in the table above are our own estimates, worked out from public information. They are not figures Qatar Airways publishes.

Gulf Air — no pilot vacancies as of September 2026

  • Ten vacancies were listed in September 2026, and none of them is a pilot, captain or first-officer role.

CAREER GUIDE

Which of the five to choose

1
You are moving with family and want housing and school fees in the comparison
Emirates

The only one of the five that states the value of company accommodation as a figure. It also publishes a total that assumes "employee, spouse and 2 eligible children", so the closer your family is to that assumption, the more readable it is on paper. But the captain door is also the highest of the five: 7,000 hours and ICAO English Level 5+.

2
Your hours are still short, or you fly the A320
Etihad Airways

It publishes two doors for first officers, and states officially that 1,500 total hours is acceptable with 1,000 hours on the A320. Age limits (under 50 for first officers, under 59 for captains), the figure and the requirements all sit in one posting, which makes it the easiest of the five to verify.

3
You are looking for "the highest-paying Gulf carrier"
We do not rank them

Only two of the five publish a figure, and those two are not counting the same things (housing in kind versus a cash allowance). Any article that gives a ranking is including estimates. What this page can show is each carrier’s range and where it came from. These ranges narrow only as more pay data arrives from pilots flying there.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which Gulf airline pays pilots the most?+
We do not rank them. Only two of the five publish a pay figure at all — Emirates and Etihad Airways — and those two are not counting the same thing. Emirates publishes a recruitment example: for a direct entry captain, annual take home cash of about $157K and an annual pay-and-benefits total of about $323K that includes housing, schooling and other items. Etihad Airways publishes a ceiling in its job posting: about $184K a year for an A320 captain, based on 75 flying hours a month — a maximum, not an average. Qatar Airways and Gulf Air have no official figure, so the captain cells carry our own estimates — about $182K for Qatar Airways and about $170K for Gulf Air, worked out from public information and not figures the airlines publish. Riyadh Air has no official figure either; the range shown for captains ($226K–$315K) comes from pay data submitted anonymously to this site together with public material. There is no defensible "average" figure.
Do Gulf airlines publish pilot pay?+
Only Emirates and Etihad Airways. Emirates lists three figures for each rank — annual pay and benefits, annual take home cash, and a monthly amount — in both AED and USD. Etihad states in its job postings that a first officer can earn "up to AED 513,732 (USD 138,318) per year based on 75 flying hours per month". We found no figure on the sites of Qatar Airways, Riyadh Air or Gulf Air.
What is the difference between the three Emirates figures?+
They cover different things. Annual take home cash is basic salary plus flying pay annualised at 85 flying hours a month — the cash that actually arrives. Annual pay and benefits adds company housing, education, medical, travel and provident fund on top, and assumes "employee, spouse and 2 eligible children". The value of the housing itself is stated separately again. The three do not add up to one another, so which one you call "salary" changes the number by more than a factor of two.
How does housing differ between Emirates and Etihad?+
In kind versus cash. Emirates provides company accommodation and states its value separately (AED 290,000 for captains, AED 225,000 for first officers). Etihad pays a cash housing allowance, and that allowance sits inside the "up to AED 513,732" figure together with basic salary, flying pay and layover and meal allowances. Because one is in kind and the other is cash, they cannot be added or subtracted as if they were the same item.
Is the Gulf really tax-free?+
Etihad states "tax-free" explicitly in its job postings, in the sense that the country of employment does not deduct income tax. Whether it is tax-free for you also depends on your nationality and residence status, so what the official pages claim only goes as far as "no income tax withheld locally". The ranges on this page are treated as amounts with no income tax deducted.
What are Riyadh Air’s pilot requirements?+
They are not published as of September 2026. The two pilot requisitions that used to exist both redirect to the careers home page, so the postings have closed. Flight hours, licence, English level, age limit, medical and the tax-free package are also unpublished, which is why they are not shown on this page.
Is Gulf Air hiring pilots?+
Not as of September 2026. Ten vacancies are listed, and none of them is a pilot, captain or first-officer role. On pay, the careers page says only "Competitive salary package." — no figure. The GAPTU collective agreement has a July 2024 announcement but no published figures.
