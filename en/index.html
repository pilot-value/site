Pilot Salary and Career Move FAQ

Compiled from the questions working pilots actually ask

How much do pilots earn?
Pilot pay varies widely by airline and by rank. In Japan, the going range is roughly $88K–$132K for a First Officer and $138K–$220K for a Captain. The average for an ANA or JAL Captain is about $170K. At overseas carriers such as Emirates and Qatar Airways, average Captain pay reaches $182K–$233K (tax-free). At the major U.S. carriers (Delta, United, American) Captains average around $377K, and it is not unusual for senior Captains to peak above $503K.
How much do ANA pilots earn?
ANA pilot annual salary runs to roughly $88K–$132K for First Officers and $138K–$220K for Captains (averaging about $170K). ANA (All Nippon Airways) is Japan's largest airline and pays at the top of the industry. With bonuses and allowances included, total compensation for a senior Captain can reach $220K. See full ANA pilot salary details →
How much do JAL pilots earn?
JAL pilot annual salary runs to roughly $88K–$132K for First Officers and $138K–$220K for Captains (averaging about $170K). JAL (Japan Airlines) operates an extensive international and domestic network, and is known for stable employment and a well-developed training system. See full JAL pilot salary details →
What does it take to become an airline pilot?
There are three main routes into an airline pilot career.

① Company-sponsored cadet program: The airline covers the entire cost of training. ANA, JAL and others take on several dozen cadets a year, and competition can exceed 100 applicants per place.
② Self-funded training (flight school): Train at an overseas flight school, then job-hunt. Expect costs of around $126K–$189K.
③ Experienced-hire recruitment: Moving across from another operator or from the JSDF. Licenses already held and logged flight hours are what count.

Credentials required: a Commercial Pilot License (CPL) or Airline Transport Pilot License (ATPL), an instrument rating, and English proficiency (ICAO Level 4 or above). See pay by airline →
How do I move to an overseas airline as a pilot?
The overseas carriers recruiting Japanese pilots most actively are Emirates (Captain average $233K, tax-free), Qatar Airways ($182K, tax-free), Singapore Airlines ($214K) and Cathay Pacific ($226K).

Middle Eastern and Asian carriers are hiring Japanese pilots aggressively, and you can apply with an ATPL, the required flight time (typically 1,500–2,500 hours or more) and English proficiency (ICAO Level 4 or above). Benefits are generous too, including housing allowance, medical cover and staff travel. See pay at overseas airlines →
How much does pay increase on upgrade from First Officer to Captain?
On upgrade from First Officer to Captain, annual salary typically rises by around $57K–$63K. At ANA, pay moves from First Officer (average $113K) to Captain (average $170K), a step up of about $57K, and JAL shows the same pattern. Overseas, upgrade criteria are clearer still: at Emirates a First Officer on $176K moves to $233K (tax-free) as Captain, so the jump at upgrade is far larger.
