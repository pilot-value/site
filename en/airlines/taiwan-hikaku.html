OFFICIAL FIGURES

EVA Air is the only one of the three to publish a pay figure

Across the three airlines’ recruitment pages, annual reports and statutory filings, the only pay figure published is on EVA Air’s English recruitment page. Where nothing is published, the cell says so rather than being filled with an estimate.

BR

EVA Air — first-officer pay published as a figure

“Annual income exceeding USD 100,000, effective from your first operational flight.”
Source: EVA Air recruitment page (English, first officers)
  • It is stated for first officers. Allowances and subsidies are calculated separately, and a performance-linked year-end bonus, a housing subsidy or company housing, eight consecutive days off a month and 22–42 days of annual leave are all listed on their own.
  • The contract runs four years.
  • This is a floor; no ceiling is published. The about $100K shown for a first officer on this page is that official floor itself.
  • ⚠️ The regional editions differ. en-global, en-us and en-th carry this USD wording; en-sg, en-gb, en-au and zh-hk still show older Taiwan dollar figures; zh-tw, ja-jp, ko-kr and vi-vn carry no figure at all.
  • The Taiwan dollar figures are not used here. No language edition says whether they are monthly or annual.
  • The captain figure of about $110K on this page is our own estimate, worked out from publicly available information. It is not a figure EVA Air publishes.
  • Separately: APAS, a pilot recruitment agency, lists first officers (A321 / B777 / B787) at NTD 2,880,000–2,916,000 a year, and NTD 166,500–168,500 a month during training (as at September 2026). These are an agency's advertised figures, not figures published by the airline. We do not multiply the training monthly by 12 or 13 to make an annual figure. They are separate from the official figure above, so we do not blend the two into an average either. We do not convert them into yen, because we could not establish who they apply to or how long the posting runs.
CI

China Airlines — pay not published; the bond is, for each entry route

“軍方退役機師：按飛時經驗支薪；完訓合格後敘任為副機師。服務年限至少四年。”
(Military retirees: paid according to flight-hour experience; appointed first officer after training; minimum four years’ service.)
Source: China Airlines pilot recruitment (Chinese)
  • It says pay follows flight-hour experience, but no amount appears anywhere.
  • The English annual report (162 pages) gives only a rate: "The average salary for all employees of the Company increased by about 4.36% in 2025." Bonuses are given as the formula in Article 41 of the collective agreement — one month of base pay at year end, plus a separate 20% of pre-tax profit when there is a profit, with employee remuneration at no less than 3% of pre-tax profit — again with no amount. Headcount was 11,642 at the end of 2025, with no breakdown by job, so the number of pilots is not stated either.
  • The annual report confirms a collective agreement was signed with the Taoyuan pilots’ union on 29 December 2021, but the text of that agreement is not public.
  • The figures of about $126K for a captain and about $69K for a first officer on this page are our own estimates, worked out from publicly available information. They are not figures China Airlines publishes.
  • Separately: a third-party site reports that a recruitment advert for a hiring roadshow held in Kuala Lumpur in July 2026 stated USD 10,000 a month, allowances included. We could not confirm it on the airline's own pages, so it is not used for the annual pay on this page. Nor do we multiply it by 12 to make an annual figure — it is one past advert, and not the same thing as what was actually paid that year.
JX

STARLUX — pay not published; the statutory filing is an all-employee figure

  • The recruitment pages state no pay. What they do publish is flight hours, licences, English and the medical.
  • The STARLUX figures in the table above — about $195K for a captain and about $123K for a first officer — are our own estimates, worked out from publicly available information. They are not figures STARLUX Airlines publishes. Taiwan pays thirteen months a year, and a housing allowance of NTD 30,000 a month and NTD 1,000 a month of transport are separate.
  • The STARLUX estimates run $173K–$245K for a captain and $116K–$129K for a first officer. Where you sit inside that range is set by the aircraft type, years of service and the routes you fly. Income tax applies in Taiwan, and every figure here is before tax.
  • As a listed company it does file. For financial year 2025 the mean salary of full-time non-managerial employees is TWD 1,115 thousand and the median TWD 722 thousand.
⚠️ This is not pilot pay
It covers every job type outside management, including cabin crew, ground staff and engineering. The median (722) sits well below the mean (1,115) because the larger job groups pull it down. Pilots are likely to be on the side that lifts the mean, but that cannot be verified, so it is not turned into a number. Note also that the same filing reports "benefits" (1,312, the all-employee mean including managers) separately from "salary" (1,137 on that same basis); they are different lines, and benefits reads higher.
Source: Taiwan Stock Exchange open API (listed-company ESG disclosure, human capital)
For reference: the government publishes a ranking, not an amount

The Ministry of Labour’s occupational wage survey states that among detailed job categories, aircraft pilots have the highest monthly earnings and are among the highest on an annual basis. What is published, though, is the ranking and thresholds ("above NT$100,000", "above NT$1.5m") — not the pilot figure itself.

Source: Ministry of Labour occupational wage survey (2024)

NATIONALITY & BOND

The bigger difference is not pay — it is nationality and the service bond

Pay at the three airlines is not published in a form that can be lined up side by side. What differs a great deal is whether a foreign national can apply at all, and for how many years you are tied — and both of those are published explicitly.

Only one route is open to foreign nationals
All five categories live on the Chinese portal, and all five require Republic of China nationality and household registration. The English portal is the single route open to foreign nationals. The English requirements are published in detail too: cadets need 140 or above in both TOEIC Speaking and Writing, plus one of IELTS 6.0, TOEIC 750 (with Listening 400+), TOEFL ITP 527 or Linguaskill 160. Applications to the same category are capped at two.

STARLUX and EVA Air: requirements written for foreign applicants

JX
Publishes separate first-officer minimums — 1,500 hours for foreign nationals, 1,000 for domestic applicants. The wording assumes foreign applicants. No bond is published.
BR
States a four-year contract on its English recruitment page, alongside a housing subsidy or company housing, 22–42 days of annual leave and eight consecutive days off a month. It is the only one of the three that states a figure.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which Taiwanese airline pays pilots the most?+
We do not rank them: lining up figures of different kinds is not a comparison. For none of the three could we find a captain figure on an official page (checked 28 September 2026). Our own estimate for STARLUX is about $195K for a captain and about $123K for a first officer — worked out from publicly available information, not figures the airline publishes. What EVA Air publishes is a floor only — "annual income exceeding USD 100,000" for first officers on a four-year contract, about $100K — and our own estimate for an EVA Air captain is about $110K. For China Airlines our own estimate is about $126K for a captain and about $69K for a first officer — again worked out from publicly available information, not figures the airline publishes.
Do Taiwanese airlines publish pilot pay?+
Only EVA Air does. Its English-language recruitment page states, for first officers, "Annual income exceeding USD 100,000, effective from your first operational flight." STARLUX and China Airlines give no figure on their recruitment pages, in their annual reports, or in their statutory disclosures.
Can foreign nationals fly for Taiwanese airlines?+
China Airlines requires Republic of China nationality and household registration in all five categories on its Chinese-language application portal. The English-language portal is the only route open to foreign nationals. STARLUX publishes separate first-officer minimums — 1,500 hours for foreign nationals and 1,000 hours for domestic applicants — so it clearly expects foreign applicants.
Is there a bond or minimum service period?+
China Airlines states one per category on its Chinese-language page: seven years for company-sponsored cadets, four years for commercial-licence holders from general aviation, three years (or until retirement age) for experienced airline pilots, and four years for military retirees. EVA Air states a four-year contract on its English recruitment page.
Is the average salary in Taiwanese listed-company filings pilot pay?+
No. Listed companies in Taiwan must disclose the mean and median salary of full-time non-managerial employees, which covers cabin crew, ground staff and engineering as well. STARLUX disclosed a non-managerial median of TWD 722 thousand for financial year 2025 — that is not pilot pay.
Which China Airlines flight-hour minimum is correct?+
The English and Chinese portals differ. The English portal asks captains for 5,000 total hours including 1,000 PIC hours on commercial jets; the Chinese "airline pilot" category asks for 3,500 total and 800 PIC. Foreign nationals can only apply through the English portal, so the English figures are the ones that apply to them.
How many times can you apply?+
China Airlines states on its Chinese-language page that applications to the same pilot category are limited to two. The other two airlines do not publish a limit.
