Reviewed by a line pilot: the verdict

Captain and first officer averages are not published by either ANA or JAL

So this page publishes no gap, no multiple and no take-home comparison between the two. The only pay that can be traced to a company document is JAL's average annual pay for flight crew — captains and first officers together — and the starting monthly pay in each cadet posting. What can be compared is fleet, routes, alliance, cadet entry and days off, and that is what follows, as the airlines state it.

Whose pay, what it includes, and when it is from

Where the figures on this page come from

Neither ANA nor JAL publishes an average for captains alone or for first officers alone. Neither has a vacancy notice for licensed pilots — ANA runs no experienced-pilot opening at all, and JAL's licence-holder route says only to ask the training organisation you are enrolled with — so no official document states a figure for either rank. JAL's statutory annual report gives the average for flight crew as a whole and breaks nothing out by rank; ANA's contains no pilot figures at all.
The averages and ranges by rank, the year-by-year progression, the gaps and the take-home figures shown on this page previously had no record of which document they came from. What these figures assume
  • JAL's annual figure is the average for flight crew as a whole, captains and first officers together. Neither airline publishes a separate average for either rank.
  • It covers the JAL group on a consolidated basis, not Japan Airlines alone — the filing says so in its note.
  • The airline states the method: cash-equivalent pay within consolidated personnel costs, divided by the average headcount on the books during the year. That is a pre-tax basis. The note does not say whether bonuses and allowances are included.
  • The trainee figures are the starting monthly pay at each airline, not figures for captains or first officers. ANA's is the basic monthly figure only: family, housing and night-work allowances and three bonus payments a year are listed as payable, with no amounts given.
  • ANA labels its figure "planned for FY2026, updated 16 March 2026". It is not a confirmed figure for those joining in FY2027, and the posting does not say whether it is before or after tax.
  • ANA offers about 65 places and JAL about 50 for FY2027, and both state around ten days off a month once flying. ANA's base after line qualification is Tokyo.
  • The two airlines' figures are different kinds of figure — one is an average from a statutory filing, the other starting pay from a job posting — so we publish no gap or multiple between them.

How to choose

ANA vs JAL — which one should you choose?

ANA

ANA suits you if…

  • ✦The Star Alliance route network (Lufthansa, United and the rest) appeals to you
  • ✦You want to build experience on the broader international network (90+ countries)
  • ✦You want time on an unusual type — the A380 on the Honolulu route
  • ✦A bold, innovation-driven corporate culture speaks to you
  • ✦You want broad fleet exposure, from the domestic A320 to the international B777
  • ✦Being based in Tokyo once you are line-qualified fits the life you want
JAL

JAL suits you if…

  • ✦The Oneworld route network (British Airways, American, Qatar Airways and the rest) appeals to you
  • ✦You want to fly a state-of-the-art type like the A350 (first introduced in Japan by JAL)
  • ✦You want a way in that is open to experienced hires as well as new graduates (around 50 places in total for FY2027)
  • ✦You put weight on the airline disclosing the average annual pay for its flight crew every year
  • ✦You identify with a long-established, tradition-minded culture and history
  • ✦You are coming to flying after a first career (career-changer route)

Verdict: pay cannot decide this — routes, fleet and the way in can

Neither airline publishes an average by rank, so this page takes no view on which pays more. What company documents do let you check is the route network, the fleet, the cadet route in and the days off once you are flying. Going into selection, what counts is being able to say in your own words why ANA, or why JAL — both are looking for people who are serious about the job. FAQ

ANA vs JAL: frequently asked questions

Which pays more for pilots, ANA or JAL?+
We do not say which is higher. Neither airline publishes an average for captains alone or for first officers alone, and our own figures by rank read "Under review". What can be traced to a company document is the average annual pay JAL reports for its flight crew as a whole — captains and first officers together — in its statutory annual report: approx. $126K for the year ended March 2025, group consolidated. That is not a captain's figure, and not a first officer's. ANA publishes no flight-crew average at all.
Which is easier to get into, ANA's or JAL's company-sponsored cadet program?+
For those joining in FY2027 the postings state around 65 places at ANA (new graduates) and around 50 at JAL (new graduates and experienced hires combined). The two are counted differently, so no ratio or ease of entry can be read from them. Neither airline publishes how many people apply.
Which airline gets you to captain faster, ANA or JAL?+
Neither airline publishes how long the upgrade takes. The 15 to 18 years and 15 to 20 years on this page are guides only, and they move a great deal with individual assessments, medical status and each carrier's fleet requirements. The spread runs to several years, so we do not present either airline as faster. Neither company offers any guarantee that you will make captain early.
Do ANA and JAL run direct-entry hiring for licensed line pilots?+
As of 2026, neither ANA nor JAL advertises direct-entry recruitment for serving pilots (CPL/ATPL holders). At both carriers the main ways in are the company-sponsored cadet program or junior license-holder recruitment. Line pilots weighing a move to a foreign or low-cost carrier are better advised to look at foreign carriers such as Emirates and Singapore Airlines.
Whose staff travel discounts are easier to use, ANA's or JAL's?+
Both run discounted-ticket schemes for staff and their families. ANA employees also have ready access to discounts across Star Alliance carriers (Lufthansa, United, Thai Airways and others), while JAL employees can use discounts across Oneworld (British Airways, American and others). Choosing on where you like to travel is one perfectly good approach.
